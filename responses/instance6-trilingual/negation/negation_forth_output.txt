=== 1. Forth has THREE negations, none of them logical === 
  invert -1 -> 0 
  invert 0  -> -1 
  0= 0      -> -1 
  0= 5      -> 0 
  negate 7  -> -7 
  invert flips 64 bits; 0= tests; negate is two's complement. 
  'not' was REMOVED from ANS Forth: the committee could not agree 
  whether it meant invert or 0=. The seam was so real they 
  deleted the word rather than pick a side. 

=== 2. The well-formedness seam: invert on a non-flag === 
  5 invert -> -6 
  5 is 'true' by convention [nonzero], but invert 5 = -6: ALSO 
  nonzero, ALSO true! Bitwise not of a truthy value stays truthy. 
  THE LIAR DISSOLVES: x = invert x has no solution in bits, but 
  x = [x 0=] has none either, while 'truthy x = invert x' is 
  SATISFIED by every value except 0 and -1. On flags-by-convention 
  the liar is not even a constraint. Sloppy truth cannot detonate. 

=== 3. The involution seam: which nots undo themselves? === 
  5 invert invert -> 5 
  5 0= 0=         -> -1 
  invert invert = identity, ALWAYS -- bit algebra, like BQN's 1-x. 
  0= 0= is NOT identity: 5 -> 0 -> -1. It NORMALIZES: collapses 
  truthy to canonical -1. Exactly Prolog's \+ \+ : a proof-test 
  composed with itself loses information [which nonzero it was] 
  and returns the CANONICAL witness. Forth's 0= 0= is \+ \+ in bits. 

=== 4. De Morgan seam: holds for bits, breaks for flags === 
  bitwise: 12 10 and invert -> -9 = -9 ...bit-perfect 
  flags:   5 3 and 0=       -> 0 vs 0 
  5 and 3 = 1 [nonzero=true] so NOT[5 and 3] = false: correct ONLY 
  because 5,3 share bit 0. Try 5 2: 
    5 2 and 0=  -> -1  [5 AND 2 = 0: 'false'!] 
    but both 5,2 are truthy: logical AND should be TRUE, not-AND false. 
  SEAM: bitwise and on flags is not logical conjunction unless flags 
  are canonical [-1/0]. Two truthy values can bitwise-and to 0. 
  Same lesson as BQN sec 3: export logic to another algebra and the 
  laws hold only on the well-formed subdomain -- Forth does not even 
  warn you; the machine has no notion of 'malformed flag'. 

=== 5. negate's seam: the value with no negation === 
  most-negative int: -9223372036854775808 
  its negate:        -9223372036854775808 
  negate maps INT_MIN to ITSELF: two's complement has one more 
  negative than positive. A fixed point of negation -- the Liar's 
  x = not x -- EXISTS IN THE HARDWARE, at the edge of the number 
  circle. BQN put the liar's fixed point at 1/2 by algebra; the 
  machine puts one at -2^63 by wraparound. Every negation that is 
  total on a finite domain MUST have a fixed point or a defect: 
  pigeonhole. Truth-negation avoids it only by being partial.
